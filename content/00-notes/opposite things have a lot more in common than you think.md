For us to perceive two different concepts or entities as "opposite," we first require that they have at least something in common. This (or these) shared trait(s) serve as a basis for comparisons to be made. For example, if you asked a kid (because kids are quite intuitive) who is the opposite of "mother", he or she would answer "father", even though they both are parents, and are both blood-related to a children. However, if one follows through with semantic analysis, he or she might end up with a different kind of opposite--having no trait in common, and in our example, this opposite of "mother" would be "nephew."
